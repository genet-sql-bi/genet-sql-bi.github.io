Medicare Supplier Assignment Acceptance

Who accepts Medicare assignment, where, and how accurately can we predict it? An end-to-end project on the CMS Medical Equipment Suppliers directory: data-quality review, cleaning, feature engineering, a calibrated classification model, automated tests and a two-page Power BI dashboard.

Nearly half of Medicare equipment suppliers (27,707 of 56,898) don't accept assignment, so patients can be charged more than the Medicare-approved amount. Acceptance varies sharply by provider type and enrollment cohort, so outreach should be targeted, not blanket.

Data quality & rigor

04 / VALIDATION

Decisions that protect the results from bad data and over-claiming:

  • Flag, don't delete: 5,390 suspected placeholder dates kept and flagged; deleting them would remove 9% of suppliers and bias results (they accept less: 43.6% vs 52.1%)
  • Leakage review on flagged features before trusting the model
  • Calibration by risk band within ±2.6 pts, so probabilities can be used directly for ranking
  • Cohort effect reported as a hypothesis, not a cause: top feature tenure_days has Spearman ρ = −0.08
  • 8 automated tests on pipeline output: row counts, unique IDs, probability ranges, split sizes, headline metrics
  • Row reconciliation documented between the July and September CMS extracts

The dashboard

05 / POWER BI

Built as a PBIP project, so the report and model are version-controlled as text. Techniques include a threshold parameter driving TP/FP/FN/TN measures, a SWITCH-based map metric selector, Top N limited to states with 100+ suppliers, and alt text on every visual.

Business cases

06 / ACTIONS
Case 1

Raise acceptance where it's lowest

Target the 27,707 non-accepting suppliers, starting with the lowest-acceptance states and provider types.

Case 2

Convert suppliers who look like acceptors

At the 0.70 threshold, 263 test-set suppliers are predicted to accept but don't, about 1,315 directory-wide. They resemble acceptors, so they're the most likely to convert.

Why predict it

Score suppliers before their status is known

The model ranks new suppliers by how closely they resemble acceptors. The value is in the ranking and the gaps, not the prediction itself.

Limitations

07 / LIMITS

Stated up front, so the results aren't read as more than they are.

  • Single snapshot (September 2026): no trends or time forecasts
  • Association, not causation: the model can't say why a supplier declines
  • Over-predicts the 2020, 2021 and 2026 enrollment cohorts (+15 to +18 pts)
  • Directory data reflects what suppliers state, not actual billing behavior
